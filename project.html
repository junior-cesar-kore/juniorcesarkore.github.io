03 / Process

From problem to working system.

Document the engineering process in the order it actually happened: define the problem, establish requirements, develop and analyse concepts, build prototypes, test against measurable criteria, iterate from evidence, and record the final outcome.

01

Problem definition

State the problem clearly and explain its context. Identify who or what is affected, the existing limitation, why the problem matters, and the specific engineering objective. For the fonio machine, this meant addressing the dehulling process and developing a machine that could perform the operation more effectively than the existing approach.

02

Requirements and constraints

Translate the problem into engineering requirements. Record functional requirements, expected performance, dimensions, available power, materials, cost, safety considerations, environmental conditions, manufacturability, and project limitations. Separate what the system must do from constraints imposed by available components, tools, time, or manufacturing resources.

03

Research and investigation

Document the information gathered before committing to a design. Include existing solutions, component datasheets, mechanisms, operating principles, previous attempts, measurements, references, and observations from practical work. The purpose is to establish why particular mechanisms, components, communication methods, or materials are being considered.

04

System architecture

Break the project into subsystems and define how they interact. For a robotic system, this can include mechanical structure, actuation, power, sensing, control, communication, and software. Show the flow of commands and data, identify interfaces between subsystems, and establish what each subsystem is responsible for before detailed design begins.

05

Concept development

Generate possible solutions and compare them against the requirements. Record sketches, mechanism choices, component selection, calculations, assumptions, and trade-offs. Explain why the selected concept was chosen rather than simply presenting the final design. This is where engineering decisions should be connected to performance, simplicity, cost, reliability, and manufacturability.

06

Detailed design and modelling

Turn the selected concept into a buildable system. Include CAD models, dimensions, assemblies, drawings, circuit diagrams, wiring, PCB or breadboard layouts, control logic, software structure, and component specifications. Record important calculations such as loads, torque, speed, dimensions, power requirements, tolerances, or communication parameters used to justify the design.

07

Simulation and analysis

Use engineering analysis to identify problems before or during fabrication. Record the model assumptions, boundary conditions, calculated or simulated results, and what those results mean for the design. In the fonio machine development, simulation was used to investigate the machine design and helped reveal that a straight shaft arrangement was not suitable for the chamber length, leading to a small shaft incline.

08

Prototype and fabrication

Describe how the digital design became a physical system. Document manufacturing methods, 3D printing, machining, bending, welding, soldering, assembly, wiring, programming, and any changes required during fabrication. Record differences between the CAD design and the manufactured part rather than hiding them; these differences are valuable engineering evidence.

09

Subsystem testing

Test individual subsystems before combining the complete system. Define the test setup, input, expected behaviour, observed result, and pass/fail condition. For the rover, this included testing motor control, ESP32 communication, MQTT publishing and subscribing, and local-network response before progressing toward an online communication system.

10

Integration and system testing

Combine the validated subsystems and test the complete system under realistic operating conditions. Check whether mechanical, electrical, software, sensing, communication, and power systems work together as intended. Record response time, repeatability, operating range, failures, unexpected behaviour, and any dependency between subsystems.

11

Failure analysis and iteration

Document what did not work and the evidence used to diagnose it. Identify the probable cause, corrective action, and result of the next iteration. The goal is not to present a perfect development process, but to show how testing informed engineering decisions. For the rover, successful local MQTT testing provided a baseline before moving to the more difficult long-distance communication stage.

12

Validation and final evaluation

Compare the completed system against the original requirements and measurable success criteria. State what was achieved, what remains incomplete, and what evidence supports the conclusion. Include test results, measurements, photographs, videos, graphs, or observed performance where available. Distinguish clearly between a feature that was demonstrated and one that is only planned.

13

Engineering lessons

Record what the project taught you about design, manufacturing, electronics, programming, simulation, testing, teamwork, or project planning. Highlight lessons that would change the way you approach the next design. Practical work on the fonio machine, for example, showed the importance of designing with manufacturing conditions in mind rather than treating CAD and fabrication as separate stages.

14

Documentation and next iteration

Close the development record with the final configuration, drawings or code revision, test evidence, known limitations, and recommended next steps. State what you would improve with additional time, resources, or testing. This creates a traceable engineering record from the original problem through each design decision, prototype, test, failure, modification, and final result.
